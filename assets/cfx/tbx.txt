# tSync — patrol cars that flash together, on whatever non-ELS lighting you already run

GTA runs every emergency vehicle's light pattern off a timer that starts when its siren comes on. Two cars that lit up a second apart flash a second apart for as long as they are lit, and nothing in the game ever pulls them together. It is the detail that makes a line of patrol cars look like a line of separate patrol cars.

tSync restarts those timers together.

## Features

- **Works with what you already run** — vanilla lights, Luxart Vehicle Control, Ultimate Lighting Controller, and the rest of the non-ELS world. tSync does not replace your lighting script, it re-times it
- **Nothing to operate** — no keybind, no menu, no command an officer uses. There is one diagnostic and that is the whole surface
- **Runs with zero edits** — all four options ship with a default, and a key you delete comes back as the value that shipped
- **No server component to the sync, and no network traffic** — see below, because this is the interesting part
- **Stays out of the way** — a vehicle another lighting script is driving is skipped by default
- **Free, and readable** — MIT licensed, every file in the download editable, no encrypted file and no escrow

## How it works, and why it is this small

Two facts about FiveM do most of the work.

**The reset is per-observer.** Toggling a vehicle's siren flag restarts GTA's light timer for that vehicle *on the client that does it* — including vehicles that client does not own. So the fix is applied to what you are looking at, and there is no ownership to negotiate. There is no case where a car cannot be synced because somebody else is driving it.

**The clock is already shared.** Every client has `GetNetworkTimeAccurate()`, which the engine keeps synchronised. tSync does not pick its own moment to re-phase; it waits for the next multiple of the configured interval on that clock. Every client reaches that instant together **without being told to**.

Put together: no net events, no server component to the sync, and no dependence on latency. A player on a 300 ms connection re-phases at the same instant as one on 20 ms, because neither is waiting for a message.

> **The alternative, and why it is not used.** Reset, then broadcast the reset to nearby clients. That is the natural design if you have not noticed the clock is already shared — and once you have, the round trip buys nothing except a dependency on ping.

## What it will not do

- It will not switch lights on or off, or change a pattern, a colour or a flash rate. It only re-times what is already running
- It is not a lighting controller. No UI, no keybinds, no patterns
- **It cannot sync a script that renders its own LEDs.** There is no engine timer behind those to restart. tSync is for vehicles GTA is lighting

## Configuration

Four settings in `config.lua`, commented in full: how often to re-phase, how far out to bother, which vehicle classes count, and whether to defer to another lighting script.

Validated at boot — a mistyped key is a line naming the key, the file and the fix, not a setting that silently did nothing. Your file is never rewritten.
